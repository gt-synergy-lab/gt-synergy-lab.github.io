    Abstract

    Large language models (LLMs) have significantly transformed the landscape of artificial intelligence, demonstrating exceptional capabilities in natural language understanding and generation. Recently, integrating LLMs with neurosymbolic architectures has emerged as a promising approach to enhance contextual awareness, interpretability, and planning capabilities. However, this integration presents substantial computational challenges that hinder scalability and efficiency, particularly in edge computing environments. This paper provides an in-depth analysis of these challenges and surveys state-of-the-art solutions, focusing on memory-centric computing principles across both algorithmic and hardware layers. Our exploration centers on the key computational elements of the Transformer, the foundation of modern LLMs, and the vector-symbolic architecture (VSA), a leading neurosymbolic model for edge applications. Building upon this analysis, we propose potential research directions to enable scalable, efficient, and cognitively inspired LLM–VSA integration. By examining these aspects, this paper aims to bridge critical gaps toward achieving effective artificial general intelligence at the edge.
